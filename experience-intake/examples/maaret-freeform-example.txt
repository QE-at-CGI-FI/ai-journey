I work as a tester across several client engagements. Day to day I use Edge Copilot,
Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, Rovo, and UiPath. I've tried ChatGPT and Claude 
Cowork, Claude, and Codex, as well as Ollama with local models. 

For generating ideas on what to test, I lean on AI to answer questions and
give me perspectives I wouldn't have thought of myself. When there's a
public bid to summarize I work through it locally, moving files and
findings around myself; for things like CGI AI Exchange metrics, and
analyzing open source projects for testing purposes, I do more systematic
online research with AI. I build Rovo agents and UiPath agents directly
inside the tools I already use for client work. I've also been
experimenting with AI notetaking — it struggles across multiple channels of
communication it can't see, and with the routine of the tooling itself.

AI regularly expands what I take on beyond my usual scope — fixing bugs is
a good example of something I now do that I used to leave to developers. I
built a CV experience visualizer, a personal application I wouldn't have
built even if I'd had the time otherwise. I treat twenty years of my own
public authoring as source material for a kind of second brain. I keep an
eye on token usage — Codeburn, token reporting on pull requests, both local
and organizational use. I use AI to generate artifacts like bid responses,
and I've pitched AI use cases into projects, the MH Java upgrade being one
example.

I've had code autocomplete since GitHub Copilot in 2021. I use reactive
agentic work for summaries, personal applications, and teaching testing.
I select and configure third-party MCPs myself — the Figma, Storybook and
Playwright combination is one I've put together — and I integrate
third-party AI system components into my setup as well. I use skills and
AI-friendly, git-first templates, like the accessibility feedback slice and
TIM.

I watch over others doing proactive, automatically-starting agentic work,
and others running unattended dark-factory style pipelines, even in places
where I'm not the one running it. I use spec-driven development frameworks
— OpenSpec, BMAD, Speckit, and a few self-chosen templates of my own.

At the team and product level, both ORT and MH have real shared context set
up that the whole team draws on. That's not true everywhere though — the
more advanced version of team/product context, building my own MCPs, AI
feature delivery for a proof of concept, and AI features actually running
in production are things I've seen at some clients, just not yet in any of
mine.

I use AI for feedback on product features too — ORT's two 25k-bug efforts
and the Omni Raha agentic slices are examples. I've delivered AI-native
projects end to end: ORT as a greenfield build, MH as a brownfield one. I
mention my AI use in my CV. And I run local models — Qwen-coder and
Devstral — through both GitHub Copilot and Claude Code.
